# The workflow engine and the application core cycle (T2.5)

## Goal
A volunteer or an official creates a beneficiary application on the project's placed form, with the person found by NID or birth registration, saves it as a draft and sends it to the office; the officials review it, edit it where needed, send it for approval and approve or reject it; every step runs through the configurable workflow engine with its permission, guards, effects, audit and notification, and everyone sees the application's timeline.

## Problem
Phase 2 so far gives the beneficiary master (T2.2), the form engine (T2.3) and the forms built by the Monitoring Team and placed by the PM (T2.4 to T2.4c). The workflow engine is designed (`docs/architecture/WORKFLOW_ENGINE.md`, the seeded templates, the snapshot each project takes at activation) but not built, and no one can create or review an application yet. The public form already writes entries that wait in `needs_verification` with nobody to see them.

## Requirements
- **R1** The engine reads a project's workflow snapshot (the seeded templates merged, taken at activation) and moves an application only through a transition the snapshot allows: the caller must hold the transition's permission within scope, every named guard must pass (a failed guard answers 409 with the guard's reason), the effects run in the same database transaction, the audit entry is written and the matrix's notification is published. The common states are exactly BD-11 plus `needs_verification` and `waitlisted`.
- **R2** Transitions, their permissions, guards and effects are configuration: nothing in the application service names a state change by hand, and a guard, effect or permission named by a template without an implementation is refused at seed time and at API start.
- **R3** The activity table and the bulk-update pattern (`POST /projects/{projectId}/bulk/{screen}`, at most 50 rows, an idempotency key, one result per row) are built once; in this task the officials' review list uses it for "রিভিউ শুরু" and "অনুমোদন" on several ticked applications, each row through the same permission, guards, effects and audit as a single action, a failed row never rolling back the others; reject stays one application at a time (owner, 2026-10-24).
- **R4** A new application starts with the person: the volunteer, team member, RPM or Project Executive enters the NID or birth-registration number and its kind, the lookup fills and locks the identity questions of the placed form (BD-02, the marked questions of D-126) or starts a new person, a blocked person stops the application with nothing more said to a volunteer (BD-06), a possible family duplicate warns and lets them continue (BD-04, D-116), the volunteer's own mobile is refused (BD-03), and the project's form is filled in the renderer; the answers that fill beneficiary columns create or update the profile and the family members at submission (T2.3 R8, BD-02's change log on mobile and address). Creation and submission are refused outside the application window and beyond the per-volunteer limit, which counts drafts and the team members' applications per assignment (BD-16, O-13, D-096).
- **R5** Who may do what follows the A7 table and the team-member rules (BR-041, BR-091): in `draft` the main volunteer edits, deletes and submits and a team member edits only what they created; in `submitted` the main volunteer still edits and deletes; from `under_review` the volunteer only views; a deletion is a soft delete; officials of the region (RPM, Project Executive) create and submit their own applications without a volunteer limit.
- **R6** Review: an official starts the review, edits the whole form where needed (each changed answer is logged with its old value, new value, actor and time, visible to officials), sends it for approval, and the approver approves or rejects; in house construction the PM gives the final approval as the template says (BD-24, BR-094). A rejected application can be returned to "অনুমোদনের অপেক্ষায়" by an approver with a reason, every step logged; one person keeps one application per project (owner, 2026-10-24).
- **R7** A rejection carries two texts: the office's reason, seen by officials, and a message for the volunteer, seen by the main volunteer and the team member who created the application; the official sees, while writing, which of the two the volunteer will read (owner, 2026-10-24).
- **R8** The application number (project code, year, a 5-digit sequence, e.g. `WNT-2026-00125`, BR-105) is given when the application is sent to the office; a draft has none, and the number opens the application for anyone within scope (owner, 2026-10-24).
- **R9** Every application shows its timeline: each state change with its time and comment, the actor's name for officials and never for volunteers (DS §5.18); the timeline is the audit trail of the application and nothing is written twice.
- **R10** The officials' screen: the application list of a project with filters (status, area, volunteer, flags, waiting more than N days, the search by name, number or mobile) kept in the address, a split view on a computer and cards with a full-screen detail on a phone; the detail shows the header with the waiting time, the signals (family duplicate with links, needs-verification flags, blocklist), the answers with the official-only sections, the photos, the timeline, the change log and a sticky action bar that offers only the actions the snapshot allows the caller now; a stale version is refused with "reload" (412).
- **R11** The volunteer's screen "আমার আবেদন": the own applications in tabs (draft, pending, approved, rejected) as cards with the person's name, the number, the village and the status, and a detail with the answers without official-only sections, the photos, the timeline and the rejection message; a draft is edited, sent or deleted from there; a team member sees only what they created; a blocked volunteer sees none (BD-06).
- **R12** Scope rules of the application permissions hold on every read and write: REGION for the RPM and the Project Executive, PROGRAM for the PM, SELF for a volunteer, `own_created` for a team member, ASSIGNED and PROJECT for the roles that hold them; outside the scope a record answers 404; the official-only sections, the scores and the reviewer texts never reach a volunteer.
- **R13** The public entries waiting in `needs_verification` appear in the officials' list with their flags and can be rejected with a reason; linking them to a profile waits for the next task.
- **R14** The notifications of the matrix are sent: submitted to the region's reviewers (coalesced per volunteer per hour), reviewed to the approvers, approved and rejected to the owner volunteer and the creating team member.
- **R15** Every transition is tested positive and negative per role, every guard's refusal is tested, the timeline is tested for its order and its hidden actor names, and an end-to-end journey creates an application on a phone, sends it, and an official reviews and approves it on a computer; `docs/api/openapi.yaml` describes every endpoint.

## Scope
- In: the engine (definitions from the snapshot, the transition service, the guard and effect registries, audit, notifications), the activities table and the bulk pattern with its first use on the review list, the application service (create with lookup, draft, edit, delete, submit, number), review and approval, the two reason texts, the reversal of a rejection, the timeline and the change log, the three screens, the scope rules, the tests and the contract.
- Out (not now): the priority score, quotas and the waitlist (T2.6; the `score.compute` effect records nothing until criteria exist); field unlock after approval (T2.6); linking a needs-verification entry to a profile (T2.6); the transfer of applications (T2.6); the offline outbox and sync (T2.7; the create endpoint already takes the client id); the stages after approval, the today's screens and photo rules (T3.1); exports (T2.8); counts of applications on the My Work home (proposed for T2.6).

## Key decisions
- **BD-11, BD-48, BD-17**: the common cycle with `needs_verification` and `waitlisted`; states, transitions and guards are configuration (BR-090). Governs R1, R2.
- **Q-04, Q-05, Q-06, BR-041, BR-091**: review by the Project Executive or whoever the RPM grants, approval by the RPM, full-form edit during review and no correction step, the team-member rules. Governs R5, R6.
- **BD-24, BR-094**: PM final approval in house construction through the template's guard, never a role name in the template (BR-193). Governs R6.
- **BD-16, O-13, D-096**: deadline and limit as guards at submission; the limit counts drafts and team members' applications per assignment. Governs R4.
- **BD-02, BD-03, BD-04, BD-06, D-116, D-126**: the lookup fills the marked identity questions; the blocklist stops, the family duplicate warns, the own mobile is refused. Governs R4.
- **D-119, T2.3 R8**: the public entry waits with flags; the split of answers into beneficiary columns, family members and custom answers. Governs R4, R13.
- **A rejection can be reversed** (owner in chat, 2026-10-24): an approver returns a rejected application to "অনুমোদনের অপেক্ষায়" with a reason; one application per person per project stays (the unique index is kept). Governs R6. Recorded as D-131 (1).
- **The number at submission** (owner in chat, 2026-10-24): drafts carry no number; numbering follows the order the office receives. Governs R8. D-131 (2).
- **Two rejection texts** (owner in chat, 2026-10-24): the office's reason and the volunteer's message; the volunteer and the creating team member read the message only. Governs R7, R11. D-131 (3).
- **Bulk review start and approval from the list** (owner in chat, 2026-10-24): the bulk pattern's first use; reject stays one by one because it needs its texts. Governs R3, R10. D-131 (4).
- **Nobody decides on an application they brought** (owner in chat, 2026-10-24, on the plan's security review): the person who created or submitted an application cannot approve, reject or return it from rejection; they may review it and send it for approval. The same rule holds for the two global roles. Governs R6. D-131 (5).
- **The timeline is the audit log** (Claude's reading of WORKFLOW_ENGINE §3 "prior state kept" and BR-179; no second history table): the engine writes the old and new state, the actor, the reason and the comment into the audit entry, and the timeline reads those entries as a projection per audience: an official sees the actor, the reason and the comment; a volunteer sees the state, the time and the volunteer's message only, never the office's reason, a return reason or the stored old and new values. Governs R9.

## Technical decisions
- **The engine lives in `apps/api/src/workflow/`** (constitution §13 "domain logic in one place"; WORKFLOW_ENGINE §13): `registry.ts` holds the guard and effect registries by name; `engine.ts` exposes `transition(tx, viewer, application, action, input)`: it finds the transition in the project's `workflow_snapshot` (the state the application is in, the action's target), checks the caller holds the transition's permission with a scope that reaches the application (`access.scopeOfUser` + `applicationWhere`), runs the guards in order (the first failure answers 409 `workflow.guard.<name>` with the guard's reason key), applies the effects in the same transaction, writes one audit entry (`old: { status }`, `new: { status, stage }`, the reason and the comment) and publishes the notification the effects name. Action names are the contract's `ApplicationAction` enum (`submit`, `review`, `send_for_approval`, `approve`, `reject`, `waitlist`, `pull_from_waitlist`, `delete`, `verify_link`, `reject_unverified`) plus `return_to_review`, which U3 adds to the contract with the reject request's `message`; `waitlist`, `pull_from_waitlist` and `verify_link` answer 409 `workflow.action_not_built` until T2.6. The mapping action → (from, to) is the engine's one table, written once.
- **Every name a template uses is built or declared** (R2). Guards built in this task: `project.active`, `project.not_closed`, `deadline.open`, `limit.not_exceeded`, `identity.present`, `mandatory_fields.present`, `beneficiary.not_blocked`, `volunteer.not_blocked`, `consent.accepted`, `actor.not_creator`, `approver.has_permission` (the construction PM rule), and `score.comment_if_against_order` and `zakat.eligible_if_zakat_fund` as guards that pass while a project has no scoring criteria and no zakat fund source (their real checks come with T2.6 and T4). Guards declared for later tasks (registered, refusing with `workflow.guard_not_built` if reached; no transition of this task reaches them): `project.setup_complete`, `template.project_close_guards` (the lifecycle keeps its own code until T3), `receipt.confirmed`, `photos.required_per_rule`, `photos.not_pending_sync`, `stock.balanced`, `stages.all_completed`, `evidence.photo_present`, `exam.passed`, `payment.recorded`, `vouchers.verified`, `site_verification.recorded`, `field_unlock.open`, `workers.assigned`, `animals.allocated`, `donor.notified`, `animal_photos.complete`. Effects built: `audit:*` (one entry per transition), `notify:*` (U5), `score.compute` and `score.snapshot` (record nothing while the project has no criteria), `quota.increment` (records nothing while the project has no quota rows), `lock:fields` (the approved state locks the answers; the effect records the lock in the audit entry), `ledger.post:approved` (records nothing until the budget ledger of Phase 3; the audit entry lists it under `effectsPending` so the gap is visible); the approve transition reaches exactly these five and the reject transition `score.snapshot`, `audit`, `notify`. Effects declared for later: `waitlist.enqueue`, `waitlist.dequeue`, `needs_verification.link`, `unlock:field`, `lock:photos`, `lock:vouchers`, `ledger.post:<other buckets>`, `outcome.set:*`, `profile.status:*`, `stock.recompute`, `activity.create:*`, `activity.close:*`, `alert.raise:*`, `donor.email:*`, `applications.restore_prior_state` (the lifecycle's own code stays until T3). The seed (`seed/sets/base/workflows.ts`) and the API start (`createContainer`) fail on a guard, effect or permission name that is neither built nor declared, the way the permissions registry fails on an unknown permission.
- **The `workflow` permission marker passes the access middleware after authentication** (`holdsPermission` in `access/scope.ts` answers false today; it answers true like `entity`), and the service decides per action from the snapshot (NE-SEC-03 in the service). The route declares `permission: 'workflow'`, `scope: 'application'` and `workflow: { scope: 'application', action: 'path' }` as the contract already says.
- **The templates change to version 2** (`_common.yaml` gains `rejected → reviewed` with `permission: application.approve`, `requires_reason: true`, `effects: [audit:application.reopened, notify:application_reopened]`; every template file bumps its `version`, since `workflow_transitions` is append-only, D-060); the demo seed's draft project points at `workflow:distribution:2`. Projects already activated keep their version-1 snapshot (BD-10, WORKFLOW_ENGINE §12) and so offer no reversal; the dev and test databases are reseeded.
- **One migration** (`20261025090000_application_decisions`, down.sql): `applications.rejection_message TEXT NULL` (the volunteer's text, R7; `decision_comment` stays the office's reason), `applications.identity_kind TEXT NULL CHECK (identity_kind IN ('nid','birth_reg'))` (the kind the lookup asked, carried on the draft so a 17-digit number is filed as the applicant said, closing the T2.3 open item), and an index `applications (project_id, status, created_at DESC)` for the drafts of the list. No status-history table: the timeline reads `audit_log` by entity (index `audit_log_entity_type_entity_id_created_at_idx`) through a reader scoped to the application, never through the global audit viewer (`audit.view`).
- **Answers are written once** (reuse): the public-forms write of `application_answers` moves to `apps/api/src/applications/answers.ts` (`writeAnswers(tx, applicationId, versionId, answers)`, replacing by field and repeat index) and `public-forms.ts` calls it; the review edit diff compares old and new answers and writes `field_changes` rows with `kind = 'review_edit'` (BR-096 shape: old value, new value, actor, time), one per changed answer. The beneficiary columns come from `split()` of `@css/shared/forms`: at submission a new person gets a profile through `BeneficiaryIdentityService.createProfile` and a known person's mobile and address go through `BeneficiaryContactService.update` (BD-02's change log); the family members through the same split's rows.
- **The person is the server's to resolve** (NE-SEC-03): the create and the submit take the number and its kind from the identity answer and resolve the profile on the server with the T2.2 lookup rules (the scope and the daily out-of-region cap of `beneficiary.lookup`, `assertMayApply`, the blocklist); a `beneficiaryId` in the body is ignored. A known person's mobile and address change at submission only through `BeneficiaryContactService.update`, which logs the change (BD-02); the create endpoint and the bulk endpoint take the `default` rate class per address like every write, and the lookup keeps its own class. Photos: the application's photo answers are upload ids the caller uploaded (T2.1 binds an upload to its uploader and `files/records.ts` decides who reaches a file through the record it links to); an application's photo is reachable by whoever reaches the application.
- **Scope**: `applicationWhere(scope)` in `access/scope.ts` follows `beneficiaryWhere`: GLOBAL everything; PROGRAM and PROJECT through the project; the areas through `region_location_id`, `district_id` and `union_id` of the application; SELF the applications whose owner volunteer is the caller's volunteer row or which the caller created (a team member's own records); the revoked projects left out (D-120). The `own_created` condition already reads `createdBy`. Every read and list goes through it; outside it a record answers 404.
- **One application per person, also before the profile exists**: a draft for a new person holds its number in the identity answer only, so the create checks the project's live applications (not deleted, not rejected) for the same number in their marked identity answer and refuses a second with 409 `application.person_taken`; the submit maps the profile's unique violation and the applications' unique index (`core/db-errors.ts`) to the same 409. A rejected application leaves the index's way (the person may be applied for again only through `return_to_review` of that application, since the index excludes nothing but `deleted`; the owner's decision keeps one application per person).
- **The limit on a return from rejection**: a rejection frees the owner's slot and `return_to_review` takes it back (the counter moves with create, delete, rejection and return); the limit guard does not run on the return, since an official's correction of a wrong rejection is not the volunteer's new application, so the count may stand one above the limit until the next decision.
- **The number** comes from `id_sequences` row `app:<projectId>` (created with the project) with `UPDATE … RETURNING` inside the submit transaction: `<code>-<year>-<00001>`, BR-105 and `@css/shared` `ids.ts`.
- **Limit and deadline as guards** at create and submit (R4): `limit.not_exceeded` counts through `project_volunteers.applications_counted` of the owner's assignment (a team member's draft counts on the lead's assignment, D-096) and the counter moves with create, delete, rejection and the return from rejection; `deadline.open` reads `apply_from`/`apply_to` and the project's extension; officials' own applications count on nobody.
- **Bulk pattern** (API_STANDARDS §13): `POST /projects/:projectId/bulk/:screen` in `apps/api/src/routes/bulk.ts` with `screen = review` now (`action: start_review | approve`), `Idempotency-Key` through the existing idempotency module (`apps/api/src/idempotency/`, the one `uploads.ts` uses; `sync_batches` stays for the offline sync of T2.7), the stored row keyed by caller, project and screen with a hash of the body and the per-row results (replayed unchanged to the same caller; the same key with another body answers 422; another caller's key is a fresh request), at most 50 rows, each row carrying the application's version (`updatedAt`) so a row changed since the list was read fails with the 412 code, each row through `engine.transition` in its own transaction, results `done | failed` with the code. The activities table gets its service (`activities.ts`: record and list by application) and `GET /applications/:id/activities`; no activity is created by the review bulk (an activity is field work).
- **Notifications**: `apps/api/src/notifications/application-events.ts` publishes `application_submitted` (recipients: holders of `application.review` whose scope reaches the application, `about: <volunteerId>:<hour>` so one entry per volunteer per hour), `application_reviewed` (holders of `application.approve` reaching it), `application_approved`, `application_rejected` and `application_reopened` (the owner volunteer's user and the creating team member's user); the catalogue entries, the bn/en templates in the seed and `notifications:sync`.
- **Web**: one page component `ApplicationFormPage` drives `LookupField` then `FormRenderer` (audience `volunteer` or `official`, `layout="auto"`, the T2.1 upload adapter for private photos) and is mounted at `/my-projects/:projectId/apply` (volunteers) and `/admin/projects/:projectId/applications/new` (officials); the draft is created on the first save and then patched with If-Match; "আমার আবেদন" at `/my-applications` joins the volunteer's phone tabs (`tabsFor`); the officials' list at `/admin/projects/:projectId/applications` with `list-query.ts` and `use-tab-view.ts`, the detail at `/admin/applications/:id` (split view from 1024 px, full screen on a phone) with the set's `Timeline` (`showActor` for officials) and `BulkList` for the ticked rows. The action bar renders `availableActions` of the read; every action with a reason opens a `ConfirmDialog` with the texts; the reject dialog has the office reason and the volunteer message with a line saying which one the volunteer reads.
- **Personal data in the change and audit rows**: the old and new answer values in `field_changes` and `audit_log` are the record BR-096 and BR-179 ask for; they are read only through `GET /changes` (official sections) and the global audit viewer, never through a volunteer's read, and pino never logs an answer value (the request logger carries ids only). A soft-deleted draft keeps its answers (BD-46: nothing is hard-deleted); the retention question of unverified public entries stays open as recorded in STATUS.
- **The plan's IDs stay in the plan**: the code, its comments and the test names say the behaviour in words (a refusal names its guard by the guard's name, never a rule number); the R, U, BD, BR and D numbers of this file never appear in `apps/` or `packages/`.
- **Stack profile** unchanged (CLAUDE.md "Stack profile"); no new dependency.

### U3. Submit, review, decide, the number, the timeline and the change log

## Risks and rollback
- **Migration:** one additive migration with a hand-written `down.sql` (two nullable columns and an index; the reverse drops them).
- **Templates at version 2:** `workflow_transitions` is append-only, so the new rows sit beside version 1; a project activated on version 1 keeps its snapshot and offers no reversal of a rejection until it is re-activated, which the lifecycle never does; the dev and test databases are reseeded (`pnpm db:seed`).
- **Separation of duties:** the `actor.not_creator` guard is configuration in `_common.yaml`; a template that drops it would let an official decide on their own application, so the seed test asserts the guard sits on every decision transition of every template.
- **Auth:** the `workflow` marker passes the middleware, so the service's permission check is the only gate for actions; U1's and U3's refusal tests (403, 404 per role) and the authorization matrix prove it before the screens exist.
- **Personal data:** the volunteer's reads mask the official sections, the actor names, the office reason and the change log; U2, U3 and U6 test each mask.
